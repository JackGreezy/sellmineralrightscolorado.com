Sell Mineral Rights in Firestone, CO

Firestone is one of the fastest-growing towns in the Wattenberg field, and mineral ownership here sits at the intersection of dense horizontal drilling and increasingly close residential development.

Weld County's oil and gas rules, including flowline and setback requirements, have tightened meaningfully over the past several years as towns like Firestone have grown closer to existing infrastructure. That tightening has generally made operators more careful about new permitting near town boundaries, without shutting off development in the wider unit areas around it.

For owners, the practical effect is that older, already-producing units near Firestone keep paying on schedule, while new development close to town moves more slowly and cautiously than it used to.

Producing units versus new permits

An interest already inside a producing Wattenberg unit near Firestone prices off its decline curve the same as anywhere else in the field. The added regulatory scrutiny near town affects mainly new permitting, not wells already drilled and online.

Setback and flowline awareness

Weld County and the state now require more conservative setbacks and flowline integrity standards near occupied buildings than in years past. This mostly affects where and how quickly new wells can be sited close to residential areas, which we factor into how we price undeveloped acreage close to town.

What we need to price your interest

A copy of your division order or recent check stubs lets us confirm your net revenue interest and current unit status quickly. Without those, we can still search Weld County records, though it takes a bit longer.

Reading a division order for a growing area

A division order tied to Firestone-area acreage can list interests across several units if your tract has been folded into more than one pad over the years. We review the full document rather than a single line, since it is easy to underprice an interest by missing a second or third unit.

Anchor the Area to the Colorado Record

An area brief should identify the local property context without substituting a city or county label for tract evidence. The file connects the local tract to the legal description, ownership fraction, formation, producing unit, operator, lease position, paid decimal, statement history, and nearby activity. County records, state well information, payor documents, and the owner file may each answer a different part of the schedule. The review should preserve those source differences rather than smoothing them into a generic statewide summary.

Compare Local Tracts Through the Same Evidence Lines

A local tract can be compared with other Colorado interests only after the same evidence lines are populated: recorded right, paid interest, production, decline, lease burdens, inventory signals, title risk, and written offer scope. Basin proximity, operator activity, or a nearby completion can add context but does not guarantee development. A useful written comparison states the date of the evidence, the assumptions included, the property excluded, and the title or timing condition that could still change the result.

Does living close to Firestone's growth affect your producing interest?

Not directly. A well already drilled and producing prices off its decline curve regardless of nearby residential growth.

Are new wells still being permitted near Firestone?

Some, though setback and safety requirements have tightened, which can slow timelines for acreage close to town boundaries.

What if your mineral interest has never been leased?

We price it against comparable Weld County lease and sale activity, adjusted for the added scrutiny new permits face near developed areas.

How quickly can we close?

Typically two to four weeks once title is confirmed against Weld County records.

What if your division order lists more than one unit?

We evaluate each unit separately and combine them into a single total offer for your complete interest.
